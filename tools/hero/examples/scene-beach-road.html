How it was made · about 1 hour with the hero-building tools

From public data to a model the game can use

THE GAME TODAY

A frame from the running game

Dev build, high quality, from the south-east over Beach Road. The same meshes are exported into stage 1.

STEP 1 · AUTOMATIC

Outlines and LiDAR

OSM has one outline per building: Scene One, Two and Three, 12, 14 and 15 levels. Inside each one the LiDAR shows two clear levels: tower roofs at 46–50 m and podium decks at 8–15 m.

STEP 2 · AUTOMATIC

Terraces

tools/hero/sites/scene_beach_road.py classes the heights inside each outline (tower, upper podium, podium, low). It cleans them with a 3 m mode filter and traces them at 0.5 m into polygons clipped to the outline, each with its median roof.

STEP 3 · NEEDS A HUMAN

Facades from the street

Mapillary images from Beach Road show white slab edges with balconies, angled lime-green fins and the curved white west end. They also show the teal glass of Scene One's round bay, a dark retail colonnade and the green cladding of the podium on the car-park side. Listing photos show the tennis court and pool on the podium.

What the hero model is made of

Measured, read off a photo, or guessed

MeasuredEvery terrace's footprint and roof height, the ground level under each building (LiDAR); the outlines (OSM).
From street photosBalcony bands, the colonnade, the round bay's glass, the podium's green cladding, the fins' colour.
GuessedWhere the green fins stand along each facade and how far they rise, the storey height (3.2 m), the colonnade's height (5 m), the walls nobody photographs (inside the block).
Putting it in the game

What's left after this review

Shape dataThe terraces are plain prisms: add them to the LINZ buildings file as building parts, which the game already extrudes, and drop the three blocks they replace.
FacadesThe game's building shader draws windows per face. It would need a balcony-band style and a green fin colour, as Spark Arena added its ribs.
A cheaper pathThe terrace step is generic: run it over every LINZ building whose LiDAR spread is wide, not just these three.
