How the two layers stack

The Domain first, the museum on top

  1. Each layer has its own recipe and frame. auckland_domain.py measures the park in a 1.5 km box; auckland_museum.py measures the museum in a 260 m box. Both frames are NZTM metres, so moving the museum into the Domain's frame is a translation of m, with no rotation or scaling.
  2. The top layer declares its footprint. museum_model.json carries the outline and its origin. The Domain reads it with --exclude, and that one outline is the only place the boundary is defined.
  3. The base layer keeps clear. No trees or park buildings within 3 m of the footprint, no crown reaching over it, and the photo under the footprint plus 2 m filled with the forecourt's tone. The forecourt and the Court of Honour stay as photo.
  4. Both stand on one ground. Heights are absolute (NZVD2016). The museum's walls start at the lowest LiDAR ground inside its outline, m, so it meets the Domain's ground with no gap and no float.
  5. In the game, both move together. The game's terrain is not the LiDAR. Every part moves by the game's height minus the LiDAR ground at its own anchor. Each tree moves at its trunk, each park building at its centroid, and the museum by one value at its centroid so its roofs stay level.
The museum layer

Measured, read off the mesh, or guessed

MeasuredRoof levels as LiDAR terraces (main block m above the ground, the raised centre m). The atrium dome's surface is LiDAR at 1 m, top m. The portico's top, and the outline, dome and columns from OSM.
From the meshEvery facade and the roof. Auckland Council's 2023 textured mesh is projected straight onto four side planes and the roof plan, and each wall takes the side it faces.
GuessedThe entablature's depth (4.2 m) and the column diameter (60 % of the OSM ring). Inner courtyard walls take the outer facade's projection. Glass is drawn opaque.

What the port has to do

MuseumReplace the box in buildMuseumAndObelisk with the terraces, the dome heightfield and the portico. Facade textures go in an atlas, about 1.5 MB as WebP. Collision is the terraces' prisms.
DomainBake the trees into a binary next to the port stacks, and lift the "no trees on the aerial" rule inside the park. Swap the park buildings in through applyHeroBuildings.
OrderLoad the museum first, because its footprint is the Domain's exclusion. In a test, check that no tree or park building lies within 3 m of it and that the museum's base is within 0.5 m of meshHeightAt at its centroid.
PhotoThe game's aerial square keeps the museum's roof painted on the ground. Fill it in the baked webp, as stage 4 does.
Reading stage 4

What is and isn't the game yet

Stage 4 uses the game's high-tier terrain (43 m grid) and its 1.25 m photo with the museum filled, the trees and buildings moved onto that terrain, and the game's own CBD around them. What it can't show: the game's sky, fog and lighting, its shaders, its foliage meshes, and the level of detail it picks at range. The pass uses the game's 60° field of view.
