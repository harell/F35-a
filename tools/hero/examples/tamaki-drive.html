Tāmaki Drive waterfront: paths, seawall, lamps and trees from LiDAR

This is the first thing a player sees where the harbour stops and the land begins. Each segment is shown four ways: the game today from the same camera, the raw LiDAR, the model on the real ground, and the model against the LiDAR surface. Drag to orbit, scroll or pinch to zoom.

Can we see it

Yes, on both sides of the road, but not all the way

OpenStreetMap, the 7.5 cm aerial, the LiDAR and street-level images all agree. Paths line both sides of the road only on the city end, from The Strand over the Hobson Bay causeway to Ngapipi Road (about 2.1 km). From Ngapipi Road to St Heliers there is one wide shared path on the seawall and an ordinary footpath on the land side. Bikes ride in green-painted lanes on the road there.

What the model is made of

Measured, read off images, or guessed

MeasuredWhere each path runs and how wide it is (OSM centrelines, corrected to the edges in the aerial and the LiDAR’s intensity, which tree shade doesn’t touch). Seawall crest and toe positions and heights, and whether it is rock, concrete or a low beach wall (LiDAR DEM and aerial texture). Every lamp pole’s position, height and arm direction (point cloud). Every tree’s position, height, crown size and colour, and which ones are palms (LiDAR, point cloud, aerial). Railing lines (OSM).
From imagesThe lamp style: a grey galvanised pole with one short arm and a flat LED head. The white post-and-rail fence on the causeway. The concrete seawall’s steps at Mission Bay. Surface colours: asphalt, concrete, basalt (Mapillary, the aerial).
GuessedRailing height and post spacing (1.1 m, 2.5 m). Pole and head dimensions. Crown shapes (a squashed sphere; pōhutukawa really spread wider and lower). Arm length where the point cloud showed no head offset. The seabed under the water line.
Not modelledRoad markings, kerbs, benches, bus shelters, signs, the boat ramps, the Ngapipi bridge’s balustrade and the rail line. Mission Bay reserve beyond the drive.
