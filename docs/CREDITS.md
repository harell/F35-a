# F35-A — Credits & licences

F35-A is built almost entirely from procedural content: aircraft, SAM and ground models are generated from code,
textures are painted onto canvases at runtime, Auckland's coastline, terrain, roads, CBD buildings and the aerial photo of the CBD, the waterfront, the Devonport peninsula and the gulf islands come from LINZ open data and its airfields from
OpenStreetMap (below), and every
sound effect and the music are synthesized with Web Audio. The third-party assets below are the only exceptions.

**Geography.** The Auckland coastline (mean high water, ±10–20 m) and terrain heights (LiDAR, resampled to 86 m) are
real LINZ data, and the volcanic cones are snapped to the LiDAR summits. Native bush, pine plantations and scrub follow
the Topo50 vegetation polygons (172 m grid), and the water depth follows the LINZ nautical charts' depth areas (172 m grid,
chart datum moved to mean high water). The CBD streets, the motorway carriageways and the
main arterials are LINZ road centrelines (the Harbour Bridge abutments snapped to them). The CBD's buildings are the LINZ
outlines extruded to their 2024 LiDAR heights (±5 m), plus the towers completed since the outlines were captured (2017),
traced from the LiDAR. The Sky Tower is built from its OpenStreetMap 3D building parts (heights ±2 m, radii ±1 m); the
Harbour Bridge is measured from the 2024 LiDAR (deck profile ±0.1 m, truss top ±0.5 m) on its OpenStreetMap piers, with the
structure under the deck taken from photos. The Ports of Auckland wharves, the piers, pontoons, breakwaters and marinas,
Devonport Naval Base (buildings, Calliope Dock), the Wiri oil terminal's tanks and Eden Park's stands are their OpenStreetMap
outlines; at Fergusson the eight ship-to-shore cranes, the floodlight masts and the container stacks are where the 2024 LiDAR
found them (elsewhere the cranes and stacks are procedural), and the yachts are procedural except at Westhaven. Herne Bay
and Westhaven are measured house by house: each OpenStreetMap outline carries the gable, hip or flat roof fitted to the 2024
LiDAR (and the aerial's roof colour), the trees grow to the LiDAR's canopy (share and height per 20 m), and Westhaven's
1,788 boats and every pontoon are where the 2024 aerial shows them; both stand among their real LINZ streets. The Scene apartments on Beach
Road are their towers and podium traced from the LiDAR inside the OpenStreetMap outlines. Every CBD building taller than
Scene 3 is its LiDAR terraces (roofs ±2 m at the spot checks) inside its LINZ outline, coloured from Auckland Council's
2023 3D mesh. The houses of the Devonport peninsula, Waiheke and the gulf islands (≈ 17,000, each up to 600 m²) are
the LINZ outlines as oriented rectangles of the same area and spread with the 2024 LiDAR's eave and roof pitch and the photo's
roof colour, moved onto the photo's roofs by its measured local offset (≤ 2 m at 20 spot checks, median under 1 m). The
hospitals, shopping malls, railway stations (with their platforms beside the railway) and schools across the theatre, and the
bigger buildings of Devonport and the islands, are their LINZ outlines inside the OpenStreetMap sites, extruded to their 2024
LiDAR roof levels (±5 m at 98 % of 965 spot checks). Over Devonport, the North Shore to Takapuna, the CBD, the isthmus and the flight corridor from
Whenuapai to Māngere, and on the gulf islands, the 3D trees and the far-field suburb colour follow the 2024 LiDAR canopy (the share of
each 32 m cell's land under trees 3 m and taller, buildings taken out, and the trees' height per 128 m), on the photo too. Whenuapai, Auckland Airport, Ardmore and North Shore (Dairy Flat) are their
OpenStreetMap layouts: runways from their mapped thresholds (±2–5 m), taxiways, aprons, hangars and terminals, with the terrain
levelled along the real outline. The ground of the CBD, the waterfront, Devonport and the inner suburbs (a 5 km square from Westhaven to the
Ports of Auckland and from Devonport to the Domain) and the tops of the wharves are the LINZ aerial photo of January 2024 (1.25 m on
the high tier, 2.5 m on medium; within ±2 m of the LINZ streets and coastline), and so are the CBD buildings' roofs, each moved to where the
photo shows it (the photo leans tall roofs off their footprints; 755 of 916 registered by correlation); the procedural houses and trees stay off it.
The same photo covers the rest of the Devonport peninsula (Stanley Bay, Bayswater, Belmont, Narrow Neck, Cheltenham, North Head; same
resolution, fading out across the neck north of Belmont) and the gulf islands' land (Rangitoto, Motutapu, Rakino, Motuihe, Browns Island
and Waiheke with Pakatoa and Rotoroa; 5 m on high, 10 m on medium; within ≈ 1–1.5 m of the LINZ building outlines). The stadiums stand among their real LINZ streets, with no procedural street under them. Elsewhere the suburbs' street grid and houses are
procedural and most other landmark positions are still hand-placed (roughly 100–400 m accuracy). The hand-traced map and
roads, the procedural CBD and the template airfields (on the real runways) remain as an offline fallback; the map still names the harbours for the procedural bathymetry.
